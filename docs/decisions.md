# Decisions

Why the project is built the way it is. Add an entry when you make a trade-off, and don't reverse one without asking the owner.

| Date | Decision | Why | Revisit when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-27 | **How QuikFly earns from city rides is a setting: commission per ride (default) or a flat daily fee** (`vendorFlatDailyFee`, `vendorDailyFee`). On the daily fee a ride carries no commission, and a driver's first completed ride of an India day charges the vehicle's owner the fee once (`vendor_day_fees` guards it, the money is a `fee` ledger row in the completion batch); a day without rides costs nothing. Group-travel trips keep the commission | Owner's decision after the competitor check: Rapido, Ola, Uber and Namma Yatri have all moved to zero commission with a flat fee, and drivers now expect it; keeping both lets the pilot run on commission and switch without a release. Charging on the first completed ride avoids billing a driver who earned nothing | The agreement term still says "commission": reword it and bump `VENDOR_AGREEMENT_VERSION` before the switch is turned on for real partners; GST on the fee and a weekly or monthly plan are not built |
| 2026-11-27 | **Owner-drivers register inside QuikFly Captain with QuikFly Partner's own screens, behind `captainSelfSignup` (off); bikes are a fifth partner vehicle type** (Captain step 6). The application is the same one (`/vendor`, reviewed by staff as usual); once live, the owner adds themselves as the driver and Captain links them by their number. A bike runs on petrol or electric only; bike-taxi options (not Auto) go to bikes, with their own `liveRideBike` switch | One application and one review path for every partner, whichever app they start in; reusing the Partner screens keeps one implementation of a long, document-heavy flow. Bike taxi is the first service, so captains must be able to register bikes. Off by default so staff open sign-ups when they are ready to review them | Captain gets its own simpler owner-driver application, or partners' updates (approved, changes asked) are also shown in Captain |
| 2026-11-27 | **Riders pay for a live ride when a captain accepts, and never a cancellation fee while a new captain is being found** (Captain step 4). The website follows live rides now; the app from its next release (both send `rides=live`); the mock API stays simulated | Paying at acceptance means no refund for every unmatched search; a captain giving the ride back is not the rider's doing, so the API (not the client's reason) waives the fee. The demo build runs on the mock and must keep working without captains | Real payments (then authorise at booking, capture at acceptance) |
| 2026-11-27 | **QuikFly Captain version 1: demo locations only, online only while the app is open, offers read aloud in a voice language the captain picks** (Captain step 3). The app is a third flavor (`com.quikfly.captain`) of the same Flutter project; it builds without its Firebase file (push off until the owner adds the app to Firebase) | GPS in the background needs a foreground service, a disclosure and Play policy work (step 7), and the demo has to work away from Chennai. A captain on a bike needs offers spoken in their language even while the app's text is still English (until `featureLanguages`). A Captain build that needed a new Firebase file first would block every developer and the owner's release machine | Step 7 (real GPS, background), the owner's Firebase app for Captain, a native speaker's check of the Tamil and Hindi phrases |
| 2026-11-27 | **Live rides: the rider pays after a captain accepts; scheduled rides stay simulated; the no-captain fallback uses the demo roster** (Captain step 2). A live ride starts with no driver; `POST /bookings` is refused (409) until the ride is `arriving` or `arrived`, and a ride nobody took is refunded in full. A ride booked ahead stays simulated in this version. When nobody takes a ride and `rideSimulateDrivers` is on, a roster captain steps in (never a partner cab: its driver would not know) | Charging before a match means refunding every unmatched search; paying at acceptance is the moment the rider knows who is coming (Uber/Rapido pattern). Dispatch is driven by polls, and nobody polls a ride booked for tomorrow, so scheduling needs server-side assignment near pickup, which waits for later. A simulated partner cab would put a ride on a real driver's statement that they never drove | Dispatch of scheduled rides near pickup; real payments (then hold, capture at acceptance) |
| 2026-11-27 | **Captains' offers are pushed but never listed in an inbox**: `driver.*` notifications go only to QuikFly Captain phones (not browsers), and `GET /me/notifications` leaves them out for every client. A captain reads offers and their ride from `POST /driver/ping` | Released customer apps list every non-partner kind, so a captain who also rides with QuikFly would see their offers in the customer app. The offer has seconds to answer, so the push is sent right after the response (`waitUntil`), not at the next cron minute; the poll is the guarantee | Captain gets its own inbox (then `NotificationScope.captain` and a captain filter on the list) |
| 2026-11-27 | **Giving a ride back after accepting can cost the vehicle's owner a penalty; declining an offer never does** (`driverCancelPenaltyPercent`, 0 by default, max 10, capped at ₹100). Valid reasons (rider no-show, rider asked, vehicle problem, can't reach pickup) are always free. The penalty is a `penalty` ledger row with no booking id; a driver online in Captain gets no simulated partner rides | The 2025 aggregator guidelines allow a driver-side cancellation penalty of up to 10%, ₹100 at most; 0 for the pilot. The ledger's one-row-per-booking-and-kind guard would hide a second give-back of the same booking. A driver taking real offers must not also be handed simulated rides they never see | Penalties per reason, or a captain's own earnings ledger |
| 2026-11-27 | **QuikFly Captain: real drivers take rides, behind `featureLiveDrivers`** ([driver-app-plan.md](driver-app-plan.md)). Fleet drivers and self sign-up; city rides first; nearest captain first, one at a time; the simulated captain stays as a fallback switch; offers show the full trip and money split, are read aloud, and declining is never penalised | Rides were the last fully simulated part and need no paid service. The owner chose the scope and asked for clear differences from Rapido, Uber and Namma Yatri. Dispatch advances when a client asks (no always-on server on the free plan); only clients that declare `rides=live` get live rides, so released apps can't complete one from their own timer | Volume outgrows polling (then WebSockets on the paid plan); paid maps and SMS after sign-off |
| 2026-11-27 | **A captain is a partner's driver record linked to a normal account by its mobile number** (`vendor_drivers.user_id`, set at the first sign-in to QuikFly Captain); one number, one driver. Positions live on the demo city map (`geo.ts` places a GPS fix on it), latest only | No second login system: the number was proven at sign-in, staff approval and licence expiry already hang off the driver record, and earnings already settle to its vehicle's owner. One coordinate system means dispatch, quotes and the customer's map agree without paid maps. Keeping only the last position is the least a dispatcher needs | Real SMS OTP arrives (the link then rests on a real proof of the number); paid maps replace the demo map |

Redirect Rules can't cover workers.dev (not our zone), so the asset-only Workers get one small script, run for page requests only so static files stay free | No supported app shares workers.dev links any more (then `workers_dev` can go off for the website and admin) |

| 2026-11-26 | **The domain `quikfly.in` is the first paid item: bought by the owner at GoDaddy (3 years), DNS on Cloudflare's free plan, the three Workers on custom domains (`quikfly.in` + `www`, `admin.`, `api.`) with workers.dev kept on.** `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` stays `*` | Owner's choice before sign-off (an exception to the 2026-11-16 free-tier rule). | 2026-11-23 | **Browsers get standard Web Push (VAPID), sent from the Worker; phones keep Firebase.** The website and the admin panel subscribe through their own service worker and store the subscription JSON as the push token (`platform: web`); the Worker encrypts and signs each message (`services/webpush.ts`, `@block65/webcrypto-web-push`).
